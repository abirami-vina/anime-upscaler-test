Let’s say you've finished an anime illustration, and it looks great on your screen. But when you try to use it as a wallpaper, print it, or share it in a larger format, the limitations become obvious. Lines look rough, small details lose definition, and enlarging the image the usual way doesn't always help.

That’s the problem an anime upscaler is built for. It increases an image's resolution while trying to retain the details that make the original artwork recognizable, from facial features and hair strands to fine outlines and shading.

That said, bigger doesn’t automatically mean better. AI processing can also change small details, smooth out textures, or add things that weren’t in the original.

To find out what actually happens, we tested PixAI's Anime Image Upscaler on three types of artwork: line art, a flat-color illustration, and a detailed character illustration. We upscaled all three on November 9, 2026, using both [PixAI](https://eap.pixai.art/go/abirami) (an anime art creation platform) and another image editing tool, then compared the results.

## Does an Anime Upscaler Look Better Than Ordinary Resizing?

Making an image bigger doesn’t automatically make it look better. A standard image editor enlarges an illustration by interpolating the new pixels, while an AI upscaler tries to reconstruct details from the image itself.

To see whether that makes a real difference, we enlarged the same source images in GIMP, a free image editor, and compared them with PixAI’s outputs. In GIMP, we opened the Image menu, chose Scale Image, set the size to match PixAI’s output, and used Cubic interpolation, a standard resizing method (with no extra sharpening). ## Upscaler, HiRes, or Local Repair?

The upscaler isn’t the only way to get a bigger or better image in PixAI. Here’s a breakdown of when to use each option:

- **Anime Image Upscaler:** Use it when you already have a finished image and just want it bigger. Upload, click Generate, download. No prompt needed.
- **HiRes:** Use it when you’re still generating. HiRes is a generation-panel setting that creates a lower-resolution image first, then redraws it at a higher resolution with extra detail guided by your prompt. Its denoise strength controls how much the redraw changes, so it can alter more than an upscale does.
- **Local Repair:** Use it when just one part is wrong, like a hand or a face. PixAI’s Enhance tools, including Face Fix and Handfix, handle this. An upscale only makes the flaw bigger.

## When the Upscaled Result Isn’t Usable

None of our three results were unusable. The biggest changes we found were small ones that only showed up at high zoom: harder outlines around the eye in the detailed illustration, and a sharper earring in the flat-color one. Neither change affected how the images looked as a social post or wallpaper, so we kept the upscaled versions as they were.

Your results may differ, especially with a blurry source or very fine detail. If an upscale doesn’t work for you, start with the detail that matters most for how you’ll use the image. If an ordinary resize keeps that detail better, use the resize. A slightly softer image is often safer than a sharper one with a changed accessory.

If the problem is in the source itself, fix it before upscaling. You can use [PixAI’s enhancement tools](https://blog.pixai.art/en/pixai-image-enhancer-6-ai-image-enhancement-tools-free-ai-photo-filters/) to repair faces and hands, then upscale the corrected file. If the upscaled version changes the character too much, keep the original. An accurate smaller image is more useful than a larger one that doesn’t look like your character.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Here’s what you might still be wondering about PixAI’s anime upscaler:

- **How can I upscale anime art with PixAI’s image upscaler?** Open the PixAI Anime Image Upscaler, upload your existing illustration, and click Generate. When it finishes, download the result and compare it with your original. In our tests, the tool returned every image at 2× its original dimensions, with no scale setting to choose.
- **Does a 2× anime upscaler change the artwork?** It can. The goal is to enlarge the image, but AI processing may sharpen outlines or slightly change small details. In our tests, the overall artwork stayed the same, but at high zoom we noticed harder outlines around one eye and a sharper, more defined earring. Check the eyes, facial features, and accessories before you use the output.
- **Can an anime upscaler fix a blurry face or incorrect details?** It can sharpen soft edges, but it can’t reliably recover detail that isn’t in the original. It also isn’t a face-repair tool. If the character’s eyes, facial features, or anatomy are wrong, you’ll need a separate repair or enhancement step first.
- **How do I check the downloaded image’s resolution?** Open the file’s properties. On Windows, right-click the image, choose Properties, and open the Details tab. On a Mac, open it in Preview, click the Tools menu, and choose Show Inspector. On Linux, most image viewers have an Image Properties window. Compare the width and height with your original file.
